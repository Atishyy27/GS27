    WHAT SENIORS TELL JUNIORS

    What seniors tell juniors, and what to do before an interview.

    1. Ten tailored applications beat five hundred generic ones. Read the company, make the CV for it, mail the founder.
    2. Learn concepts, not languages: microservices, pods, scaling. Everyone needs some JavaScript, APIs and SQL.
    3. Under 200 LeetCode? Don't quote the number. Keep solving.
    4. Say what your project did, with a number. Nobody can see it ran for the whole college.
    5. Startups worry you won't show up. Say when you can join, and keep it.
    6. Walk up to seniors without hesitation. Ask one concrete question about your road.
    7. DSA gets you in the door. Development is the longer game: it never stops changing.
    8. Bangalore is always open. Off-campus is not luck if you start in second year.
    1. Three solid projects, two internships. Headline each by what it does, then explain how.
    2. Know your terms cold. Fumbling the words reads as not having studied.
    3. Prompt your own project into 15 points, then mock-interview yourself out loud.
    4. Read every line of your CV: about half the questions come from it. Carry a college-format hard copy.
    5. MNC portals rarely reply, even with follow-ups: HR filters on college name. Go through people.
    6. The 2026 graduates' market lands in 2027. "Lots of time" is the trap.
    A recent check: for one well-known company's opening, every alumnus working there has a master's. Before you apply, look at who actually works there. That is the real bar.

    LIVE: OUTREACH

    Outreach, live: write one together.

    • Casual, not formal. Subject: builder, looking for <role>.
    • Line 1: proof of work, with a link and a number
    • Line 2: why this company, specifically
    • Line 3: I can join now, zero notice period. CV attached.
    • Send it to the address on the job post. One person, not ten.
    • Follow up. The content decides replies, not the count.
    • Live demo: one written in front of you
    Hi name,

    I'm you, branch batch at SGSITS, your junior. I saw you work on team / product at company.

    I've built one proof, with a number.

    Could we talk on a call this week? When are you free?
    • Message seniors from our college, or people doing the exact work you want.
    • First message asks for a call, not a referral.
    • When they reply, call. Talking beats typing: a message is secondary for them.
    • Same college is not a free pass. It's hit or miss, so send more than one.
    • No reply? Follow up once, politely.

          APPENDIX: YOUR ROAD

          Pick your road, and what engineering means now.

          MNC or Dev? The road decides the work.

          MNC / product OA

          • Competitive programming is the filter
          • Contest rating > random question count
          • LeetCode + Codeforces contests, every week

          Dev / startup

          • Open source is the proof
          • LFX mentorship is open right now
          • Merged PRs in real orgs > tutorial projects

          Either road: finish one sheet (Striver A2Z / NeetCode 150 / CSES) before you count questions.

          Quick question: what is software development?

          Tech is compressing

          • It's an umbrella word for fifty different jobs, not one.
          • One engineer now does many of them. Know more than one thing.
          • A single intern role can mean evals, a desktop app, field visits and support in one year.

          Core is not dead

          • Embedded, VLSI, avionics, aerospace: design and test, end to end.
          • It's the future, if you commit to it.
          • Seniors can get you contacts, up to the gate. Getting in is your work.
