WHAT I KEEP TELLING JUNIORS

The same eight things, every time someone asks.

  1. Ten tailored applications beat five hundred generic ones. Read the company, make the CV for it, mail the founder.
  2. Learn concepts, not languages: microservices, pods, scaling. Everyone needs some JavaScript, APIs and SQL.
  3. Under 200 LeetCode? Don't quote the number. Keep solving. I still do.
  4. Say what your project did, with a number. Nobody can see it ran for the whole college.
  5. Startups worry you won't show up. Say when you can join, and keep it.
  6. Walk up to seniors without hesitation. Ask one concrete question about your road.
  7. DSA gets you in the door. Development is the longer game: it never stops changing.
  8. Bangalore is always open. Off-campus is not luck if you start in second year.

YOUR CV AND YOUR INTERVIEW

Before you walk into any interview.

  1. Three solid projects, two internships. Headline each by what it does, then explain how.
  2. Know your terms cold. Fumbling the words reads as not having studied.
  3. Prompt your own project into 15 points, then mock-interview yourself out loud.
  4. Read every line of your CV: about half the questions come from it (my estimate). Carry a college-format hard copy.
  5. MNC portals gave me zero replies, follow-ups included. HR filters on college name. Go through people.
  6. The 2026 graduates' market lands in 2027. "Lots of time" is the trap.
Checked today: a Target opening came up. I looked at our alumni working there. Every one of them has a master's. Before you apply, look at who actually works there. That is the real bar.
