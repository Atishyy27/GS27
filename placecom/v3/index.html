WHAT I KEEP TELLING JUNIORS

The same eight things, every time someone asks.

  1. Ten tailored applications beat five hundred generic ones. Read the company, make the CV for it, mail the founder.
  2. Learn concepts, not languages: microservices, pods, scaling. Everyone needs some JavaScript, APIs and SQL.
  3. Under 200 LeetCode? Don't quote the number. Keep solving. I still do.
  4. Say what your project did, with a number. Nobody can see it ran for the whole college.
  5. Startups worry you won't show up. Say when you can join, and keep it.
  6. Walk up to seniors without hesitation. Ask one concrete question about your road.
  7. DSA gets you in the door. Development is the longer game: it never stops changing.
  8. Bangalore is always open. Off-campus is not luck if you start in second year.

LIVE: COLD MAIL

My real count: about 125 first mails over five months. Plus follow-ups.

  • Casual, not formal. Subject: builder, looking for <role>.
  • Line 1: proof of work, with a link and a number
  • Line 2: why this company, specifically
  • Line 3: I can join now, zero notice period. CV attached.
  • Send it to the address on the job post. One person, not ten.
  • Follow up. The content decides replies, not the count.
  • Live demo: I'll write one in front of you

CELL TEA

What the cell has taught all of us. No names, promise.

  • Reviews look at how you talk, code and carry yourself. Not just the tasks you ticked.
  • The most common complaint about any batch: too casual.
  • Groupism splits fast: sixteen become eights, then fours, then twos.
  • Small courtesies get noticed. So does skipping them.
  • People go quiet once they're placed. Seniors remember who didn't.
  • Cell interviews start friendly, then dig into whatever you wrote on the form.
Patterns from Atishay's own conversations (Neo, 3 to 16 Oct). Keep it light. No names, no stories that point at anyone.
